Forensic Retrospective · Adversarial Review Loop

Anatomy of a review loop 18 captured rounds of full-surface adversarial review, run to convergence on one React/TypeScript app — reconstructed from 92 structured findings, per-agent telemetry, and the commit graph.

The loop worked — it shipped an app with no known blocking defects and a 136-test safety net. But the data shows it ran in two distinct regimes: a short, cheap discovery phase that found the real bugs, followed by an equally expensive self-review tail that mostly reviewed the loop's own fixes. This is the forensic consolidation of what happened — quantitative first, then five qualitative lenses. No remedies yet.

02 / Where the value was

Re-bucketing all 75 confirmed findings by genuine engineering value. Only 13 (17%) were user-visible bugs — and twelve of them landed by round 8.

75 confirmed findings by value bucket

The value knee is round 8, not round 12. Rounds 4–8 hold 12 of the 13 real bugs; rounds 9–21 produced 34 more confirmed findings and exactly one user-facing bug — a missing aria-pressed on the layout toggle (round 16). The largest single bucket was not bugs but consistency (31/75): copy that contradicts a gauge, two constants that could drift. The blocking flag stopped tracking user value early — the last three blocking findings were a hostile-import hardening, a perf freeze, and a test flake.

03 / The loop's own tail

Each round reviewed the previous round's commit. A finding is self-induced when it targets code, a constant, a comment, or an abstraction the loop's own fixes created — not the original product.

Self-induced share per round · rounds 5–21

The tail turned inward — it did not clean up. Self-induced share rose from 65% in the discovery era to 70% in the tail, and rounds 13–16 were 100% self-induced (12/12): every finding targeted a comment, constant, or abstraction the loop had itself added. Both endpoints were manufactured, too — the final blocking finding (round 12) was a test the loop's own round-11 fix introduced, and the loop's terminal finding (round 21) was a defect in the loop's penultimate fix.

Not all self-induction is waste. An incomplete fix that a later round completes is still progress — the reroll no-op (R7→R8) and the CSV injection escalating to a real blocking bug (R9→R10) were genuine. But by rounds 13–16 the loop was reviewing scaffolding for a feature that was never built.

04 / The four instruments

Read as a set: correctness and security were the diagnostic lenses (they found the real bugs, then correctly went silent); maintainability was the metronome that never saturates and kept the loop alive.

Critic lensFindings/round · R4→R21 ConfirmedBlockingSole-source blockingSaturation

Correctness + security caught 10 of the 13 blocking bugs (five each, seven of them sole-source — seen by no other lens). Maintainability filed the plurality (32 confirmed, 43%) but zero sole-source blocking — its two blocking finds were same-round duplicates of a correctness finding. Solid's entire value was a single irreplaceable catch: a stale-async race in useBuddyGraph (round 4) no other critic saw.

Security saturated to extinction. All 12 of its findings landed in rounds 4–11 on one enumerable surface (resource exhaustion from untrusted input + export injection); it returned nothing found for nine straight rounds afterward. That silence is the correct terminal state of an exhausted surface — this app is local, offline, no network or auth. Maintainability's surface, by contrast, is self-renewing: every fix seeds a fresh stale comment or duplicated constant. Convergence was gated by maintainability running out of nits, not by the app becoming correct — that had happened by round 12.

Redundancy peaked early. In 7 of 18 rounds two or more critics independently reported the same defect. Round 4's high: all four critics filed the same root cause — a disconnected import scoring 100% quality — so 4 of that round's 7 findings were one bug with one fix.

05 / The ratchet locked cases, not themes

Every confirmed finding was ratcheted into a test. Of 92 findings, 87 wore distinct class labels; only 4 ever literally recurred — and no ratcheted input ever re-broke across 18 rounds.

That is a flawless anti-regression lock. But it was a poor anti-theme barrier: the 92 findings collapse into ~12 themes, and 8 of them recurred across three or more rounds under new class labels. The ratchet caught the exact tuple; the theme walked around the point-test. Reviewers said so explicitly in their test-upgrade notes — "tests \t/\r only as leading chars, so it cannot catch this" (R10); "only asserts the fallback fires above the caps, never the expensive in-range corner" (R11).

06 / The stopping rule

The loop stopped when a round reported zero confirmed findings. That signal was severity-blind, grade-fragile, and gave no leading indicator.

It came one grading decision from stopping five rounds early. Round 15 reported a single confirmed finding from a single critic — had that lone suggestion been graded plausible, the rule fires at round 15. Round 16 then produced four confirmed, equal to the entire earlier plateau. The confirmed curve was non-monotonic — 1 → 3 → 1 → 4 → 3 → 3 → 3 across rounds 13–19 — then fell off a cliff to 0. Nothing in the trajectory predicted round 20.

Zero-confirmed is a grading threshold, not an absence of observations. Both "converged" rounds still surfaced a plausible finding, and both pointed at the same soft spot — the error-recovery branch in App.tsx. The critics kept seeing it; it merely landed on the plausible side of the line. The two-round confirmation (round 20 and 21) is what actually supplied robustness against the single-quiet-round fragility that round 15 exposed.

The substance was retired 8 rounds before the formal stop. Last blocking finding: round 12. The nine-round tail spent 49% of the total token budget and 41% of the wall-clock to convert "no blocking remains" into "zero confirmed remains" — a distinction the counting metric could not see.

07 / Six consolidated findings

Where the five independent lenses converge. Ranked by how strongly the data supports them.

The loop had two regimes, split at round 12

A cheap, high-value discovery phase (rounds 4–12: 57 findings, 13 blocking, 47K tok/finding) and an equally expensive, near-zero-value self-review tail (rounds 13–21: 18 findings, 0 blocking, 143K tok/finding). Every lens located this boundary independently — value knee at R8, last blocking at R12, self-induced peak at R13–16, security saturation at R12.

corroborated by all 5 lenses

Two-thirds of what it found, it had caused

56 of 84 post-baseline findings (66.7%) targeted loop-introduced code — rising to 100% across rounds 13–16. After the original code was combed (~R8–12), the "fresh eyes, full-surface" premise quietly degraded into a review of the loop's own recent diff.

iatrogenic lens

The ratchet prevented regressions, not recurrence

Zero ratcheted case ever re-broke — a genuine, durable lock. But it locked tuples, not themes: 8 of 12 themes resurfaced under fresh class labels, and half the chains were spawned by the prior fix. Passing tests gave a false sense that a class was closed.

theme lens

An asymmetric instrument set determined the outcome

Correctness and security are finite, diagnostic surfaces — they caught the real bugs and correctly fell silent by round 12. Maintainability's surface is self-renewing: every fix seeds new nits. Convergence was gated by that one non-saturating lens, not by the app's defect rate.

critic lens

The stop signal was severity-blind and grade-fragile

Counting confirmed findings could not see that danger ended at R12; it nearly false-stopped at R15 on one grading call that R16 would have refuted. The two-round confirmation, not the zero itself, supplied the robustness.

convergence lens

Value inverted against the metric it optimized

Only 17% of confirmed findings were user-visible bugs; the last "blocking" issue was a test flake; convergence coincided with the critics running out of reachable surface, not with the code becoming defect-free. The rule kept optimizing after the thing worth optimizing was gone.

08 / The five lenses, in detail

The forensic backing for the consolidation above. Each lens read the full 92-finding dataset independently.

Lens 01

Iatrogenic — did the loop create its own work?

  • 66.7% self-induced (56/84). 36 caused by the immediately-prior fix, 20 by an earlier loop-introduced construct; only 28 were pre-existing original code.
  • Rounds 13–16 were 100% self-induced (12/12) and surfaced zero pre-existing bugs — the loop reviewing LAYOUT_MODES, lazy forcePos, SEPARATION_DEFAULT, POLISH_MAX_N, FORCE_MAX_EDGES, all constructs it had itself added.
  • The reroll cascade: one genuine blocking bug (R7, seed-bump is a no-op) spawned ~10 self-induced follow-ups as each fix's new notice, callback, and detector became the next target.

Lens 02

Themes — lifecycle and ratchet effectiveness

  • 12 themes wear all 87 distinct labels. The reroll theme alone wore 11 labels for one behavioral concern; the median theme wore ~7.
  • 10 of 12 themes were born by round 6. The entire suggestion tail introduced exactly one new theme (extensibility seams, R12) and otherwise re-expressed rounds 4–6 themes under new names.
  • The loop opened and closed on the same theme — "an unusual state transition leaves the UI stale/wrong" — from R4's async-clobber to R20/R21's error-recovery, spanning all 18 rounds and never reducible to one locatable bug.
  • The hygiene theme is parasitic: 5 of ~7 stale-comment findings are debris left by other themes' fixes — the "stale-comment ×3" recurrence is one round-6 cap change falsifying three comments across three later rounds.
Lens 03

Critics — behavior, saturation, redundancy

  • Correctness + security caught 10/13 blocking bugs, 7 sole-source. Maintainability filed 43% of all findings but produced zero sole-source blocking signal.
  • Security: 12 findings → 0 across the round-12 boundary, then nine rounds of silence. Its one late finding (R21) was a follow-on nitpick on code correctness had just flagged — a saturated lens manufacturing output.
  • Solid was bimodal: one uniquely-caught blocking race (R4) justified its whole presence; the rest was design-taste suggestions with the run's worst plausible-to-confirmed ratio.
  • Maintainability never decayed — productive through R19 — because its surface regenerates faster than it can be cleared. It set where convergence landed.
Lens 04

Value — taxonomy and diminishing returns

  • 13/75 confirmed (17%) were genuine user bugs. Buckets: consistency 31, doc-hygiene 13, real-bug 13, latent-robustness 11, extensibility-speculation 6, test-infra 1.
  • The knee is round 8: rounds 4–8 held 12 of 13 real bugs; rounds 9–21 produced 34 confirmed and one user bug (R16 a11y).
  • The 13 real bugs collapse to ~6 distinct defects, and all but one live on two surfaces: file import of hand-edited files and the reroll button. Genuine value came from stress-testing the two input-taking features.
  • The nine-round tail traded ~2.6M tokens for 1 real fix, 13 internal cleanups, and 4 seams for code that doesn't exist.
Lens 05

Convergence — stopping-rule mechanics

  • One grade call from stopping at R15 (five rounds early), a stop the R16 spike-to-4 would have immediately falsified.
  • Non-monotonic: three round-over-round increases in confirmed, two inside the "converging" tail; the count plateaued at fixed values (4, then 3) like a per-round critic quota rather than a draining reservoir.
  • Convergence was a cliff: 3 → 3 → 3 → 0, no taper, no leading indicator; the rule could only run blind until the four-critic zero coincided — twice.
  • The findings pool behaved like a renewing sample (near-zero recurrence over 16 rounds of sustained yield), so "zero confirmed in one round" is the noisy joint probability of four independent samples all coming up empty.
Forensic notes & data provenance
  • Coverage: 18 of ~20 rounds. Rounds 2–3 ran (per the commit graph) but predate the captured structured outputs; round 4 is treated as the analytical baseline for self-induction.
  • Quantitative facts (counts, tokens, timing, churn, class frequency) are parsed deterministically from the 18 workflow output files and git history — reproducible.
  • Interpretive layers (value bucketing, self-induced attribution, theme clustering) were produced by five independent fresh-context agents reading the finding text, cross-checked against findings that self-describe their cause. These are judgment calls; the boundaries between "consistency" and "real-bug," or "immediate" and "lagged" self-induction, carry ±1–2 finding of ambiguity and do not change any headline conclusion.
  • Scope caveat: the subject is one small, offline, client-side app. The early saturation of the security lens is partly a property of that surface and should not be assumed to generalize.
  • Framing caveat: "self-induced" is descriptive, not pejorative — completing a genuinely incomplete fix is real progress even when the follow-up was self-triggered.
